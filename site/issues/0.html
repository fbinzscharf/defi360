The 360 Brief

Week ending Friday, November 20, 2026. On-chain finance, read the way a risk desk would read it: five things that mattered this week, and what to watch next.

1. A stablecoin reserve took a $1.5 billion withdrawal and held

More than $1.5 billion left the reserve module behind Sky's USDS stablecoin after a report traced about $102 million of North Korea-linked swaps through it. The peg held.

Why it matters. This was a run driven by compliance fear, not by doubts about collateral. For anyone holding stablecoins as cash, the lesson is that the exit door worked under pressure. The open question is what the protocol changes so tainted flows cannot use the module again.

Source: The Crypto Desk, Nov 20

3. Regulation: US agencies keep writing the rules Congress did not

The CFTC chair said on-chain trading venues must register, or partner with a registered exchange, to serve US customers. Separately, an SEC nominee told the Senate she wants the September exemption for tokenized stock trading turned into a formal rule with public comment.

Why it matters. With market-structure legislation stalled, the rules are arriving through agencies. Formal rulemaking is slower than exemptions but harder to reverse, which is what institutions need before they commit.

Source: BitDigest, Nov 20, The Crypto Desk, Nov 20

4. Tokenized Treasuries: steady at about $14 billion

The market for tokenized US Treasury bills stood near $14 billion on November 19, a little below its peak earlier this year. Franklin Templeton leads with about $3.5 billion, followed by Ondo's USDY at about $2.7 billion. Yields run at roughly 3.5% to 3.8%, tracking short-term Treasury rates.

Why it matters. This is the on-chain risk-free rate. Any stablecoin lending yield should be judged against it: the gap is what you are paid for taking protocol risk.

Source: Crypto Briefing, rwa.xyz

5. Backdrop: a weak market, and DeFi shrinking with it

Bitcoin fell below $59,000 during the week. DeFi's headline total value locked sits near $71 billion.

Why it matters. Falling prices shrink collateral and raise liquidation risk across lending protocols. Weeks like this are when weak designs get found out.

Source: The Crypto Basic, Nov 20, Coinpaprika
